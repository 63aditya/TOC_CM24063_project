Conversion convention
    
  • A → Ba gives the move B --a--> A, i.e. the RLG rule B → aA
  • A → a gives Z --a--> A, i.e. Z → aA, where Z is the new start symbol
  • A → ε gives no rule of its own: the moves of A are copied to Z
  • The LLG start symbol S becomes the final state: S → ε

    4Theory in Brief
    
  
    What is a Left-Linear Grammar?
    G = (V, T, P, S) where every production is A → Bw or A → w: a non-terminal may appear only at the left end. This tool uses the normal form A → Ba | a | ε. LLGs generate exactly the regular languages, just like right-linear grammars.
    
  
    Why is the conversion not just "flip each rule"?
    Flipping every rule A → Ba into A → aB gives a right-linear grammar for the reversed language LR, not for L. To keep L itself, the construction reverses the automaton: the flipped grammar is read as an NFA, the NFA is reversed (start and final states swap roles, arrows turn around), and the result is written back as an RLG.
    
  
    The construction used here
    Add a new start state Z. For A → Ba add the move B --a--> A. For A → a add Z --a--> A. For A → ε copy every move of A to Z. The old start symbol S is the only final state (and Z is final too when ε ∈ L). Write every move p --a--> q as p → aq and every final state f as f → ε.
    
  
    How is language integrity checked?
    Both grammars are enumerated independently by dynamic programming on word length, and the sets of all words up to a bound are compared. The "Check a String" section also derives the string in each grammar. Any difference would be reported as a mismatch.
